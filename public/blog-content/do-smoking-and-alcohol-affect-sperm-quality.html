How is Sperm Quality Determined?

Sperm quality is generally assessed on various important parameters, including:
  • Sperm count,
  • Sperm motility (movement),
  • Sperm morphology (shape and structure), and
  • DNA integrity
It is important to understand that all such factors can contribute to the ability of a man to fertilize an egg successfully. If any of these elements get disrupted, there can be infertility, lower chances of conception, or the risk of miscarriages even if conception occurs. In such cases, early fertility treatment is necessary, and consulting an infertility clinic in Kolkata can be helpful.

How Smoking Affects Sperm Health?

Cigarette smoke includes over 7,000 chemicals, many of which are well-known carcinogens and toxins. Formaldehyde, cadmium, carbon monoxide, and nicotine are some of the harmful substances that can be found in cigarettes. When these toxins enter the bloodstream, they reach the testicles, interfering with sperm production and function. According to research, smoking has been found to reduce sperm count and motility significantly. Men who smoke regularly are often found to have a lower concentration of sperm in their semen. Even the sperm that their body manages to produce are not quite capable of moving towards an egg effectively. Smoking also raises the risk of abnormal sperm production, which can reduce the chances of successful fertilization and hinder IVF success. Smoking also causes damage to the sperm DNA. According to studies, smoking increases oxidative stress in the body, which leads to the fragmentation of DNA within the sperm cells. It reduces the chances of conception as well as leads to birth defects and developmental problems in offsprings. The levels of hormones responsible for controlling sperm production are also affected by smoking. Excessive smoking has been associated to reduced testosterone levels, which can have a negative impact on the quality of erection, libido and overall sexual health. All of these can have an indirect impact on fertility, and consulting a qualified IVF doctor in Kolkatacan help assess the situation.

How Alcohol Intake Impacts Sperm Quality?

Just like tobacco, excessive alcohol consumption can impact male reproductive health. The liver is the powerhouse responsible for breaking down alcohol in the body. However, chronic alcohol intake can reduce the function of the liver and result in hormonal imbalances, which can hurt fertility. Intake of excessive amount of alcohol can impair sperm quality. It may lower testosterone while triggering a rise in estrogen levels. The process of sperm production, or spermatogenesis, can also be impaired significantly. Long-term consumption of excessive alcohol is also known to result in reduced sperm production. The testicles also get reduced in size. Heavy consumption of alcohol can also impact sperm motility and morphology. This results in a greater number of sperm with irregular shape and structure. These sperms cannot fertilize eggs quite successfully, which reduces the chances of conception. Too much alcohol consumption also increases inflammation and oxidative stress in the male body, both of which can be harmful to sperm cells. Chronic drinking can lead to a build-up of a toxic byproduct of alcohol metabolism, known as acetaldehyde. Studies show that it can damage sperm DNA. Is the Damage Reversible?

Fortunately, in many cases, the negative impact of smoking and alcohol on sperm quality can be reversed. This is especially true if the man in question is relatively young and has not been consuming these substances for quite a long period. According to research conducted by fertility specialists, it has been found that young men who give up smoking and alcohol intake often notice improvements in their sperm count, morphology and motility within a period of 3 to 6 months. This is due to the fact that the body produces new sperm every 64 – 74 days, which offers the chance to recover and reverse the damage. It is important to note that the extent of recovery usually depends on a number of factors, such as overall health, age of the man and the intensity and duration of substance abuse.
