What are the Common Causes of Male Infertility?

There are numerous factors that can lead to male infertility, including:
  1. Low sperm count
This is among the leading causes of infertility in men. A minimum of 15 million sperm per milliliter of semen is typically regarded as a healthy sperm count. When the sperm count is lower, there is very less chance of one sperm fertilizing an egg successfully. Infections, hormonal imbalances, or genetic defects might cause low sperm counts. Consulting the best IVF doctor in Kolkata can help diagnose and treat these issues effectively.
  1. Poor sperm motility
Motility of a sperm means its ability to swim toward the egg efficiently. It is another common problem of male infertility. Even with a normal sperm count, poor motility can prevent successful fertilization. An infertility specialist in Kolkata can provide targeted treatment to improve motility and chances of conception.
  1. Abnormal sperm morphology
In the same way, the structure and shape of the sperm can play a vital role in determining the success of male fertility. It can impair the ability of the sperm to get to the egg and penetrate it successfully.
  1. Poor nutrition
It is important for every man to understand how nutrition affects male fertility. The male body needs a wide range of minerals, vitamins, and antioxidants to support good sperm development. Poor eating habits or nutritional deficiencies can disrupt the balance in the body, leading to poor sperm count, quality, and motility and ultimately impacting fertility. When men consume proper nutrients, they create the right environment within their body for good quality sperm production.   1. Lifestyle choices
Other than poor diet, too much smoking and alcohol consumption, obesity, exposure to environmental toxins like heavy metals or pesticides and drug abuse can be major causes. All these can affect sperm health negatively. Sedentary lifestyles and high stress levels are also often associated to declining male fertility.
  1. Hormonal disorders
Hormones produced by the hypothalamus, pituitary gland, and testicles regulate sperm production. When there is any imbalance in any of these hormones, such as low testosterone levels, fertility can decline.
  1. Genetic issues
Genetic problems such as Y chromosome microdeletions or Klinefelter syndrome can interfere with sperm development. There may be little or no sperm production in men with such conditions.
  1. Reproductive tract infections
These include gonorrhea, chlamydia, or other sexually transmitted infections, which can lead to blockages or scarring that can hinder sperm passage. Epididymitis or inflammation of the testicles (orchitis) may also impair sperm quality.
  1. Ejaculation disorders
These include problems like retrograde ejaculation, in which semen enters the bladder rather than going out of the penis. Such issues can result from certain medications or chronic problems like spinal injuries, surgeries, or diabetes.
  1. Varicocele
This is a condition where veins within the scrotum get enlarged, potentially leading to male infertility. It can raise the temperature of the testicles, affecting sperm production and function negatively. The treatment of varicocele is often found to make affected men fertile again.

What are the Typical Male Infertility Treatments and Solutions?

Male infertility treatments differ based on the specific root cause of the condition. Assisted reproductive technologies (ART) such as IUI treatment in Kolkata (intrauterine insemination), in vitro fertilization (IVF), and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) may be used when natural conception is difficult to achieve. In many situations, lifestyle modifications are found to greatly increase male fertility. Avoiding exposure to harmful chemicals, stress management, maintaining a healthy weight, reducing alcohol intake, and quitting smoking can be some of the ways to improve sperm quality positively, and boost overall male reproductive health. Surgical intervention may be the treatment of choice for varicocele and other similar medical conditions to restore normal sperm production. Reproductive tract obstructions are frequently treated with microsurgery. Hormone replacement therapy or medications that stimulate hormone production may be used to restore fertility when hormonal imbalances are found to be the underlying problem. With antibiotic treatment, it may be possible to address all those infections that impair sperm function. Testicular sperm extraction (TESE) and other sperm retrieval techniques are used when the testicles produce sperm, but it is not present in the ejaculate. The collected sperm can be utilized in assisted reproductive techniques (ART).

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What are the leading factors behind male infertility?
Male infertility is often linked to issues such as a low sperm count, irregular sperm morphology, reduced sperm movement, hormonal disruptions, and certain genetic disorders.
  • In what ways do lifestyle habits such as smoking, alcohol consumption, and stress affect male fertility?
Hormone levels, sperm quality, and overall reproductive function can be negatively impacted due to chronic stress, excessive alcohol, and too much smoking.
  • Which diagnostic procedures are commonly used to assess male infertility?
Physical examination, scrotal ultrasound, genetic screening, hormone testing, and semen analysis with DFI are the most commonly used diagnostic tests.
  • What are the available treatment methods for men diagnosed with infertility?
Assisted reproductive technologies like IVF, ICSI, TESE surgery, hormone therapy, medications, and lifestyle changes are some of the most common treatment options.
