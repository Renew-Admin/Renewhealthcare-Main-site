What Are Fibroids and Why Do They Develop in the Uterus?

Uterine fibroids are also called leiomyomas. They are growths made of muscle and connective tissue that form in or on the wall of the uterus. The important thing to remember is that fibroids are almost always benign. They are not cancerous. In fact, the chance of a fibroid becoming cancerous is about 1 in 350, which is extremely rare. However, fibroids can range in size from a tiny seed to something as large as a watermelon. At times, they grow alone or in clusters. And, depending on where they are and how large they get, they can disrupt or affect an area of your life. Most fibroids commonly appear between the ages of 30 and 50, which are typically a woman’s reproductive years when hormone levels are highest.

Causes and Risk Factors of Uterine Fibroids You Should Know

There isn’t a single exact fibroid cause, but research strongly points to hormones. Estrogen and progesterone are the hormones that regulate your menstrual cycle and appear to fuel fibroid growth as well. Fibroids grow during pregnancy when hormone levels spike. They shrink after menopause when hormone levels fall. Besides hormones, there are other risk factors as well that increase your chances of developing fibroids.
  • Family history: If your mother, sister, or aunt had fibroids, your risk goes up.
  • Obesity: A higher body mass index is linked to higher oestrogen levels, which can stimulate fibroid growth.
  • Early menstruation: Starting your period at a young age appears to increase lifetime exposure to oestrogen.
  • Not having children: Pregnancy temporarily suppresses fibroid development.
  • Late menopause: A longer reproductive window means longer exposure to hormonal fluctuations.

What are the different types of fibroids?

So, to answer your query “are fibroids harmful?”, the answer is not generic. Where a fibroid sits; inside or on your uterus determines what it’s called and what symptoms it produces.
  • Intramural Fibroids
These grow embedded within the muscular wall of the uterus. They’re the most common type. Intramural fibroids can grow quite large and may distort the shape of the uterine cavity. In turn, this leads to heavy bleeding and pelvic pressure.
  • Submucosal Fibroids
These fibroids grow just beneath the inner lining of the uterus, pushing inward into the uterine cavity. Even small submucosal fibroids can cause significant heavy bleeding and are the most likely type to interfere with fertility and embryo implantation.
  • Subserosal Fibroids
These grow outward from the exterior surface of the uterus, pushing into the pelvis. They can grow very large. Subserosal fibroids are more likely to cause pelvic pressure, back pain, and urinary symptoms than bleeding.
  • Pedunculated Fibroids
These attach to the uterus via a thin stalk like a mushroom. They can be intracavitary (inside the uterus) or extend outside it. If the stalk twists, it can cause sudden, sharp pain. Each fibroid may behave differently, which is why treatment plans are always individualised.

FAQs

1. What are the treatment options available for uterine fibroids?

Treatment options include medications for fibroids, hormonal therapy, non surgical fibroid treatment like UAE and RFA, myomectomy, and hysterectomy for fibroids. The best treatment depends on symptoms, fibroid size, and future pregnancy plans.

2. How are uterine fibroids diagnosed through tests or imaging?

Doctors diagnose uterine fibroids using pelvic examinations, ultrasound, MRI scans, hysteroscopy, sonography, and laparoscopy. Transvaginal ultrasound is especially useful for detailed fibroid imaging.

3. Do uterine fibroids affect fertility or pregnancy outcomes?

Some fibroids may interfere with fertility or pregnancy depending on their location and size. However, many women with fibroids still conceive naturally and deliver healthy babies with proper monitoring.

4. What size of uterine fibroid is considered dangerous or problematic?

There is no fixed “dangerous” size. Even small fibroids can cause severe symptoms if located near the uterine lining. Large fibroids symptoms may include heavy bleeding, pressure, bloating, and fertility problems.

5. What lifestyle changes can help manage uterine fibroid symptoms?

Maintaining a healthy weight, exercising regularly, eating a balanced diet, reducing processed foods, and managing stress can help improve overall hormonal balance and reduce symptom severity.
