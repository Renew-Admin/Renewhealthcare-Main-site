What is Endometrium?

The endometrium is the inner lining of your uterus. Under the influence of hormones like oestrogen and progesterone, this lining thickens every month. If a fertilised egg arrives, it implants here and a pregnancy begins. If not, the lining sheds. This lining has two layers:
  • Functional layer - The part that builds up and sheds every cycle.
  • Basal layer - It stays put and regenerates the functional layer each month.

What is Endometrial Thickness and What is Endometrium Normal Size in mm?

Endometrial thickness refers to how thick the uterine lining is at any given point in your menstrual cycle. It is measured in millimetres (mm). The number changes depending on which phase of the cycle you're in.
Phase Timing Normal Endometrial Thickness
Menstruation Days 1–5 2–4 mm
Early Proliferative Days 5–7 5–7 mm
Late Proliferative Days 8–14 10–16 mm
Secretory / Ovulation Days 14–28 Up to 18 mm
Postmenopausal N/A Less than 5 mm

What Are the Reasons for Endometrial Thickening?

The endometrial lining naturally thickens during the menstrual cycle. But sometimes, it thickens more than it should. Here are the main uterus wall thickening causes:
  1. Pregnancy
Pregnancy is one of the most common reasons for endometrial thickening.
  1. Hormonal Imbalance
When oestrogen levels are high and progesterone is low, the endometrium can thicken excessively. Women with PCOS often experience irregular ovulation and prolonged oestrogen exposure, which can lead to endometrial thickening over time.
  1. Endometrial Hyperplasia
Endometrial hyperplasia is a condition in which the uterine lining becomes too thick. It carries a risk of developing into endometrial cancer if left untreated. It is most common in women approaching menopause or those with hormonal imbalances.
  1. Endometrial Polyps
Endometrial polyps are small, soft growths that develop from the inner lining of the uterus. They can cause irregular bleeding and may affect fertility.
  1. Medications and Other Causes
Certain medications, including tamoxifen (used in breast cancer treatment), can cause endometrial thickening as a side effect. Other factors include:
  • Obesity
  • Diabetes
  • Persistent high blood pressure
  • Hormone replacement therapy (HRT)
  1. Cancer
In some cases, abnormal thickening may indicate endometrial or ovarian cancer. If you are experiencing menstrual disorders such as heavy or irregular bleeding, consider seeking menstrual disorders care in Kolkata at the earliest.

FAQs

1. What is normal thickness for endometrial lining?

During menstruation, it measures 2-4 mm. In the early proliferative phase, it rises to 5-7 mm. By the late proliferative phase (around ovulation), it reaches 10-16 mm. During the secretory phase, it peaks at up to 18 mm. In postmenopausal women, a normal thickness is less than 5 mm.

2. What are the main causes of a thin or thickened endometrium?

A thickened endometrium is most commonly caused by hormonal imbalances, endometrial hyperplasia, polyps, pregnancy, PCOS, or in rare cases cancer. A thin endometrium (7 mm or less) is often linked to low oestrogen levels, poor uterine blood flow, or prior uterine surgery or scarring.

3. How does endometrial thickness affect pregnancy and implantation?

The endometrial thickness of 8-15 mm is considered optimal for pregnancy. If the lining is too thin (below 7 mm), implantation rates drop. An abnormally thick lining may also disrupt implantation if it is caused by an underlying condition. Women trying to conceive with a thin uterine lining may also be advised to explore the best IUI treatment in Kolkata if implantation challenges persist.

4. How is endometrial thickness measured?

The most preferred method is transvaginal ultrasound, where a small ultrasound probe is gently inserted into the vagina. It gives a clear, close-up view of the uterus and allows the doctor to measure the endometrial thickness in millimetres.
