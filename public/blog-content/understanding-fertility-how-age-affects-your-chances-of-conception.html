Why Age Matters in Fertility?

As people age, both sexes experience biological changes. Only about 300 or 400 of the 1 to 2 million eggs that women are born with ovulate over the course of their reproductive lives. They produce fewer and lower-quality eggs as they age. They have a harder time getting pregnant as a result. This is often when couples begin exploring IVF treatment cost and available options. While men continue to produce sperm throughout life, there is a decline in sperm motility and quality with increasing age. After 40 years of age, they experience a drop in their testosterone levels. There is also an increase in DNA fragmentation in their sperm, which can have a negative impact on their fertility. It can also increase the risk of their offspring suffering from genetic abnormalities. Seeking guidance from an experienced IVF specialist in Kolkata is highly recommended in such cases.

How Age Affects Female Fertility?

A woman’s fertility often peaks in her 20s. When she reaches her early thirties, it starts to deteriorate. At age 35, the rate of female reproduction rapidly declines. The drop in her fertility mainly happens due to hormonal changes affecting her menstrual cycle and her eggs getting older. Similarly, as women age, the rate of miscarriages rises. How Age Affects Male Fertility?

Men’s fertility decreases more gradually than women’s. Even then, age plays a big role. According to research, it has been found that men over 40 years of age suffer from higher infertility risks, as compared to their younger counterparts. There is a rise in sperm DNA fragmentation and a decline in sperm volume and quality. This can result in difficulties of conception. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What effect does a woman’s age have on her ability to conceive naturally?

With advancing age, the fertility of a woman drops, and her chances of natural conception deteriorate. By the age of forty, the ovarian reserve and egg quality have drastically decreased. The most fertile and most likely to become pregnant are those in their 20s.

2. When does a woman become significantly less fertile?

beyond age 32, it begins to drastically diminish, and beyond age 37, the decline quickens.

3. Does a man’s age have an impact on his fertility and chances of getting pregnant?

Yes, although not to the same extent as female age, advancing age in men can impact their fertility and the chances of conception.

4. Are spontaneous pregnancies still possible for women over 35, and what are the risks?

Yes, but they have a higher risk of suffering from some complications compared to younger mothers. These include miscarriage, gestational diabetes, birth defects, high blood pressure, low birth weight,and preterm birth.

5. What choices do older couples seeking to conceive have for fertility treatments?

They can choose from quite a few infertility treatment options like IUI, IVF with or without ICSI, gestational surrogacy, and egg donation. Assisted laser hatching is also used, along with IVF.

6. How can I evaluate the state of my fertility as I age?

You can talk about your worries with an infertility specialist. You might be recommended to undergo fertility tests and maintain a healthy lifestyle.
