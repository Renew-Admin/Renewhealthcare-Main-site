If you are facing tubal blockage and want to know how to prevent it?

Tubal blockage, also known as blocked fallopian tubes, is a condition related to female reproductive organs. In such a condition, one or both fallopian tubes are partially or completely blocked, making it difficult for the egg and sperm to meet.

Here’s what’s supposed to happen. Every month, during ovulation, the ovaries release an egg which travels through the fallopian tube. Around this time, if intercourse happens, then the sperm travels up through the tubes & fertilizes with the egg. The fertilized embryo travels down from the tube to the uterus for implantation.

Now, if the tube is blocked at any point, the entire process halts. That’s what tubal blockage really means. It’s a physical barrier preventing fertilization or stopping the embryo from reaching the uterus.

When both tubes are blocked, it is called bilateral tubal blockage, and natural conception becomes extremely difficult without medical help.

What Causes Blocked Fallopian Tubes: Reasons For Blocked Fallopian Tubes

The causes of tubal blockage are usually related to infection, inflammation, or structural damage. The most common causes of blocked fallopian tubes include:

  • Endometriosis, where tissue similar to the uterine lining grows outside the uterus and affects the fallopian tubes.
  • Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID), caused by untreated STIs (sexually transmitted diseases) like Gonorrhea and Chlamydia, which creates severe inflammation.
  • Previous abdominal or pelvic surgeries, leading to scar tissue or adhesions.
  • Large fibroids can sometimes block the tubes, especially if they are pressing against the tubes.
  • Hydrosalpinx where fluid builds up and blocks the tube. This is also caused by previous infections.
  • Ectopic pregnancy occurs when the fertilized egg implants itself in the fallopian tube instead of the uterus. This can damage the fallopian tubes.
  • Some sexually transmitted infections, particularly Chlamydia and Gonorrhea.

Types Of Tubal Blockage

Understanding the types of fallopian tube blockage helps doctors decide the right fallopian tube blockage treatment.

  • Proximal Tubal Blockage

This occurs near the uterus. It can be caused by tubal spasms, scarring from an inflammatory condition, or blockage from debris or tubal polyps.

  • Mid-segment Tubal Blockage

This happens in the middle section of the tube. It is caused by tubal ligation, prior ectopic pregnancy, pelvic adhesions, or endometriosis.

  • Distal Tubal Blockage 

Occurs at the end of the tube, i.e., near the ovaries. It is commonly associated with hydrosalpinx, pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) or extensive endometriosis.

  • Total Tubal blockage

When both tubes are completely blocked, it is referred to as bilateral tubal blockage.

Blocked Fallopian Tubes Symptoms

Many women with blocked fallopian tubes may not experience noticeable symptoms. But when they do, symptoms of blocked tubes may include:

  • Common Pelvic pain
  • Dyspareunia or pain during intercourse
  • Foul vaginal discharge
  • Body temperature is over 101°C (in acute cases)
  • Nausea & vomiting (in acute cases)
  • Severe lower abdominal or pelvic pain (in acute cases)
  • Infertility

Over 30% of women who are diagnosed with infertility issues have a higher possibility of damaged or blocked tubes.
A specific kind of blocked fallopian tube, called Hydrosalpinx, may lead to abdominal pain & unusual vaginal discharge, but not every woman shows these blocked fallopian tube symptoms.

Hydrosalpinx is confirmed when the tube is filled with fluid, which makes it dilated & blocked. The fluid obstructs the egg & the sperm, thus preventing fertilization & pregnancy.

How Is Tubal Blockage Diagnosed?

Diagnosis of blocked fallopian tubes typically involves imaging or minimally invasive procedures. Common diagnostic methods include:

HSG (Hysterosalpingography)

A commonly used test where dye is passed through the uterus and X-rays are taken. If the dye doesn’t move through the tubes, it usually indicates tubal blockage. 

Sonohysterography (HYCOSY)

This test uses ultrasound rather than X-rays. A small amount of fluid, usually saline or contrast, is gently passed into the uterus using a thin catheter. The doctor then observes how this fluid moves to check whether the tubes are open. 

This procedure is often recommended alongside treatments like hysteroscopy in Kolkata, as both help doctors get a clearer understanding of uterine and fallopian tube health for accurate diagnosis and care.

Laparoscopy

Is a minimally invasive procedure where a tiny camera is inserted through a small incision in the abdomen. This laparoscopy treatment in Kolkata helps the doctor directly examine the tubes and identify any blockage.

It is considered the most accurate way to confirm blocked fallopian tubes but is usually reserved for cases where more clarity is needed.

How Is Tubal Blockage Treated?

Treatment depends on how severe the tubal blockage is and whether the patient is trying to conceive.

Simple lifestyle and dietary changes can support overall fertility, but there is no strong medical evidence that they can clear blocked fallopian tubes.

In mild cases, procedures such as laparoscopic tubal blockage surgery or tubal catheterization may help restore tubal function, the results can vary from person to person. When there is extensive damage or in cases of bilateral blocked fallopian tubes, surgery is usually less effective.

For women trying to conceive, IVF for tubal blockage is usually the most effective treatment. IVF bypasses the fallopian tubes completely. The egg is fertilized outside the body, and the embryo is then placed directly into the uterus.

Complications Of Blocked Tubes 

The most common complication of blocked tubes is Ectopic Pregnancy. If a fallopian tube is partially blocked, in that case, an egg may be able to fertilize but it may get stuck in the tubes after fertilization. This results in an ectopic pregnancy. 

Ectopic pregnancy is a medical emergency and needs immediate treatment. Due to this risk, doctors often recommend IVF treatment instead of surgery for blocked fallopian tubes. 

Effects Of Blocked Tubes 

Blocked fallopian tubes are a common cause of infertility.
• Bilateral tubal blockage significantly reduces the likelihood of conceiving naturally
• If the fallopian tubes are partially blocked, then one can potentially conceive, but the chances of ectopic pregnancy increase.
• If only one fallopian tube is blocked, the blockage most likely will not affect fertility because the egg can still travel through the unaffected tube.
