Why Preconception Health Matters for Both Partners

A common misconception is that only women need to prepare for pregnancy. In reality, male fertility contributes to nearly 40–50% of conception outcomes. Sperm quality, motility, lifestyle habits, and hormonal health also play a major role. For women, the 90-day preconception window is crucial because:
  • It takes around 90 days for egg maturation, meaning lifestyle changes now impact egg health three months later.
  • Hormonal balance improves with proper nutrition and sleep.
  • Nutrients like folic acid reduce birth defects and support early embryo development.
For men, sperm generation cycles also last around 72–90 days, which means diet and lifestyle changes now improve sperm health later.

🌿 Genetic Counselling & Genetic Evaluation Before Pregnancy

Genetic health plays an essential role in a healthy pregnancy. At Renew Healthcare, couples are encouraged to undergo genetic counselling if:
  • There is a family history of inherited disorders
  • The woman is above 35
  • The man is above 40
  • There have been previous miscarriages
  • The couple has experienced previous IVF failure
  • Carrier screening has been advised
A simple genetic carrier screening can help identify if either partner carries conditions such as Thalassemia, cystic fibrosis, SMA, or other inherited genetic diseases. This ensures safer pregnancy planning and helps doctors decide whether additional steps like PGT-A or PGT-M (explained in our blog on Genetic Testing in IVF) might be recommended in the future.

  • Thyroid screening
  • AMH test (for ovarian reserve)

  • Ultrasound scan
  • Nutritional assessment

Start Prenatal Vitamins & Folic Acid

Doctors recommend starting prenatal vitamins 90 days before conception. Essential nutrients include:
  • Folic acid (400–800 mcg): Prevents neural tube defects
  • Vitamin D: Supports hormone balance
  • Iron: Prevents anaemia
  • Omega-3: Improves egg quality and supports brain development
Men also benefit from:
  • Zinc
  • Vitamin C
  • CoQ10
  • Selenium
These improve sperm motility and morphology.

Manage Weight & Adopt a Balanced Diet

A healthy BMI is one of the strongest indicators of fertility health. Both overweight and underweight conditions can disrupt ovulation, periods, and sperm quality. Focus on:
  • Protein-rich foods (paneer, pulses, eggs, chicken, tofu, fish)
  • Iron sources (greens, lentils, jaggery)
  • Healthy fats (nuts, seeds, avocados)
  • Whole grains
  • Hydration (2.5–3 litres/day)
Avoid:
  • Packaged foods
  • High sugar snacks
  • Deep-fried items
  • Excessive caffeine
Renew Healthcare also provides nutrition counselling for couples preparing for IVF or natural conception.

Reduce Caffeine, Alcohol & Smoking

These habits strongly affect fertility:
  • Smoking reduces egg count, sperm motility, and increases miscarriage risk.
  • Alcohol impacts hormonal health and increases inflammation.
  • Excess caffeine (above 200–250 mg/day) may reduce conception chances.
Cutting these out three months prior significantly enhances reproductive health.

Lifestyle Adjustments for Better Fertility

Improve Sleep Quality (7–8 hours daily)

Poor sleep affects hormones such as FSH, LH, estrogen, progesterone, and testosterone. Developing a consistent sleep schedule can significantly improve fertility.

Exercise Regularly

Moderate exercises such as:
  • 30-minute brisk walks
  • Yoga
  • Light strength training
These reduce inflammation and balance hormones.

Manage Stress Effectively

Stress increases cortisol, which impacts ovulation and sperm production. Try:
  • Meditation
  • Deep breathing
  • Journaling
  • Counselling
Renew Healthcare provides holistic emotional support during fertility care.

Track Menstrual Cycles

Understanding ovulation helps increase the chances of natural conception. Use apps or ovulation kits to track cycles and fertile windows.

Preconception Tests & Screenings to Consider

Hormone & Fertility Tests

  • Thyroid (TSH, T3, T4)
  • AMH (ovarian reserve)
  • Blood sugar levels
  • FSH, LH, Prolactin
  • Testosterone (for men)

Blood Grouping & Thalassemia Screening

These are essential to rule out genetic compatibility risks.

Genetic Carrier Screening

Recommended if:
  • Either partner has a family history of genetic illness
  • There have been miscarriages
  • You're planning IVF

Infectious Disease Screening

HIV, Hepatitis B/C, Rubella, etc. Early detection ensures a safe pregnancy journey.
