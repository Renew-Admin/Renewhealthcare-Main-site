Types of Twin Pregnancies 

The most common question about multiple pregnancies is - how does this happen? Let’s understand the process. 

Source: https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Fraternal-Twins

  1. Fraternal (Dizygotic) Twins:   This is the more common type of twinning, accounting for about 70% of all twin pregnancies.  Here's how it works:
  • Your ovaries typically release one egg per cycle. However, in fraternal twinning, your ovaries release two separate eggs during the same ovulation cycle.
  • A separate sperm then fertilises each of these released eggs. Simply put- two eggs meet two sperm, resulting in two fertilised eggs.
  • These fertilised eggs develop independently inside separate placentas and amniotic sacs. Fraternal twins can be of the same or different sexes, like any other siblings.

  1. Identical (Monozygotic) Twins:

      These twins form from a single fertilised egg.  
  • Early in embryonic development, the fertilised egg (zygote) divides into two separate cell masses. This split can occur at different stages.
  • Depending on when the split happens, the two cell masses may or may not entirely separate. This can influence whether the twins share the same placenta and amniotic sac or have separate ones. 
  • Identical twins will always be of the same sex and share similar genetic makeup.
 

  • Conjoined Twins: A Rare Occurrence:

    Conjoined twins are an even rarer form of twinning, estimated to occur in about 1 in 200,000 births.  
  • Here, the single fertilised egg splits incompletely, resulting in twins physically connected at some point, commonly at the chest, abdomen, spine, pelvis, trunk, and head. 
  • The degree of connection can vary depending on where the incomplete separation occurs.
  • Conjoined twins require specialised medical care throughout pregnancy and delivery and sometimes even separation surgery after birth.

The Role of Family History and Genetics:

While the exact cause of spontaneous twinning is still being researched, certain factors may increase the chances:
  • Family History: Having twins in your close family (mother, sister) slightly increases your odds.
  • Maternal Age: Women over 35 may be more likely to release multiple eggs during ovulation.
  • Fertility Treatments: Treatments like ovulation-stimulating medications used in IVF can increase the likelihood of multiple egg releases. High-Risk Factors in Twin Pregnancies

Carrying twins presents an increased risk for certain complications. The good news is that these risks can be managed effectively with proper prenatal care and close monitoring by your doctor.
  1. Gestational Diabetes: The risk of gestational diabetes (pregnancy-induced diabetes) is slightly higher with twins. Your doctor will likely recommend additional screening for this condition.
  2. Preeclampsia: This pregnancy complication involving high blood pressure and other issues is also a potential concern. Regular prenatal care allows for close monitoring and early intervention if needed.
  3. Preterm Delivery: The increased pressure in the uterus from carrying twins can lead to earlier delivery. Your doctor will monitor for signs of preterm labour and may recommend strategies to help prolong the pregnancy.

Diagnosing a Twin Pregnancy

  1. Early Signs and Symptoms 
While not a definitive diagnosis, some early pregnancy symptoms may be more pronounced with twins. It's important to remember that these can vary from woman to woman, and experiencing some of them doesn't necessarily mean twins:
  • Increased nausea and vomiting: 
  • Rapid weight gain in early stages: 
  • More fatigue and food aversions 
  1. Medical Diagnosis:
  • Ultrasound Findings show one or two sacs and at least two heartbeats, confirming multiple pregnancies.

Taking Care During a Twin Pregnancy

A twin pregnancy requires extra care and attention to ensure the health of both mother and babies.  Here are some key aspects:

  1. Nutritional Requirements and Dietary Adjustments:

Carrying twins requires more fuel! Your doctor or a registered dietitian can help you create a personalised plan to meet your increased calorie and nutritional needs. 

  1. Physical Activity and Rest:

  • Regular, moderate exercise is beneficial for twin pregnancies. Walking, swimming, and prenatal yoga are great options. Always consult your doctor before starting any new exercise routine.
  • Listen to your body and prioritise getting enough sleep. Twins can drain your energy, so don't hesitate to take naps and delegate tasks when needed.

Planning the Delivery:

  • Due to the increased risk of complications, twin deliveries often happen earlier than singleton births. Your doctor will carefully monitor fetal development and determine the optimal delivery time.
  • The decision of vaginal delivery versus cesarean section for twins will depend on several factors, including:
    • Position of the Babies: The ideal position for vaginal delivery is for both babies to be head-down (cephalic presentation).
    • Health of the Mother: Your overall health and potential complications will be considered.
    • Previous Obstetric History: Your doctor will consider your history of previous deliveries.
  • However, due to the increased risk factors, cesarean sections are more common for twin births. Your doctor will discuss all options and factors influencing the safest delivery method for you and your babies.
