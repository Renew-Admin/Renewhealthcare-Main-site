How to Know If You Are Having Endometriosis? 

This health problem is marked by many symptoms, which tend to vary across persons. However, the common symptoms include: 
  • Severe menstrual cramps (dysmenorrhea) 
  • Chronic pelvic pain 
  • Pain during or after intercourse 
  • Heavy menstrual bleeding or bleeding between periods 
  • Fatigue, diarrhea, constipation, and nausea 
  The pelvic lining, fallopian tubes, and ovaries are the areas that are often affected. The tissue starts acting just like normal endometrial tissue, getting thicker, breaking down, and bleeding with each menstrual cycle. This tissue lining of the uterus grows in other places outside the body.   Keep in mind that the acuteness of these symptoms does not always indicate how extensive an endometriosis you might be having. Some women with a milder variety of endometriosis suffer from acute pain, while those suffering from severe endometriosis may experience mild symptoms. In many cases, managing these symptoms effectively is crucial for improving reproductive outcomes, especially for women undergoing fertility treatments. How Does Endometriosis Affect Fertility? 

It can have a big impact on your fertility. Around 30-50% of women suffering from endometriosis may have infertility issues. The condition may lead to scar tissue and adhesions, which can obstruct the fallopian tubes. When this happens, the sperm and the egg cannot meet each other and natural conception becomes difficult.  It can also cause Inflammation. If there are inflammatory substances within your pelvis, it can affect your quality of eggs, sperm, and embryo implantation.  Then there is the problem of Endometriomas. These ovarian cysts can damage your ovarian tissue and reduce the number of healthy eggs that you have.   Even if you manage to conceive, your risks of miscarriage can increase due to endometriosis. It can also lead to higher risks of complications during pregnancy.  In such cases, genetic counselling may be recommended as part of a comprehensive fertility plan. It helps assess inherited risks and supports informed decision-making, especially when considering assisted reproductive techniques.  

How is Endometriosis Treated? 

The condition cannot be cured definitely with any specific treatment. You can manage your symptoms and improve fertility with quite a few treatment options, including: 
  • Medications: You can control your symptoms with pain relievers and hormonal therapies. 
  • Surgery: You can also remove or reduce your endometrial tissue and scar tissue with laparoscopic surgery.  
  • Lifestyle Changes: Some symptoms can be reduced or even alleviated with diet adjustments, regular exercise, and stress management.
