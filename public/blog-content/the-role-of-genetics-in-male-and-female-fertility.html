Fertility problems can get in the way of parenthood. These can affect the health of both men and women, making it impossible for them to become a father or mother at any point of time in life. As any infertility specialist in Kolkata will tell you, genetic factors in males and females can play a major role in the fertility journey of both sexes. Find out why and how the role of genetic in male and female can affect fertility  

Genetics and Male Infertility 

  When it comes to men, genes regulate sperm quality and sperm production. These can have an important role in determining sperm structure, sperm motility, and sperm count.   Genetics is also a major determining factor in hormone regulation. It can influence the production of hormones such as testosterone, which are essential for male reproductive health and sperm production.   It is also important to note that genetic conditions, such as Klinefelter Syndrome, which involves additional X chromosomes, can have a negative impact on sperm production.     Cystic Fibrosis (CF) and Kallmann Syndrome are two other genetic disorders that can impair fertility in men. In Cystic Fibrosis (CF), mutations in the CFTR gene can lead to Congenital Bilateral Absence of the Vas Deferens (CBAVD), leading to azoospermia.   A rare genetic disorder, Kallmann Syndrome has a negative impact on both the pituitary gland and the hypothalamus. This leads to an insufficiency in the hormones that are necessary for puberty and sperm production.    

Genetics and Female Infertility 

  Genes can influence how many eggs a woman is born with and how fast there is a deterioration of the number of eggs deteriorates with age. Genetic factors also have a big impact on the quality and health of eggs, including their ability to be fertilized. Genes regulate egg release from the ovary, a process that is necessary for fertility.   As in case of male, even in females, genes have an impact on the production of hormones that control the menstrual cycle, ovulation, and overall reproductive health.  Chromosomal abnormalities like Turner Syndrome that involve abnormal or missing sex chromosomes can negatively impact ovarian function. The quantity and quality of female eggs are also affected by mutations in particular genes.   

Detecting Infertility: How is Genetic Testing and Diagnosis Done? 

  Both male and female infertility can be treated in a proper, well-equipped IVF clinic with expert doctors and cutting-edge technologies. Diagnostic testing are as follows:  1.Karyotype analysis  In this test, the structure and number of chromosomes are examined, for the identification of any chromosomal abnormalities.   2.Whole Exome sequencing  The test involves proper evaluation of the protein-coding regions of the genome. It helps to detect any mutation in gene.    It is essential to understand that some fertility problems are hereditary. Genetic conditions that can give rise to infertility may or may not be inherited by succeeding generations. If there is a family history of infertility or any other genetic disorders, there can be a higher risk of genetic factors that can contribute to infertility.   

FAQs: 

  • How do genetics influence male and female fertility? 
It can play a major role in both male and female fertility, affecting sperm count, sperm motility, egg quality, ovarian reserve, sperm production, and overall reproductive function. 
  • Can genetic disorders cause infertility in men or women? 
Yes, according to Renew Health Care infertility specialist, genetic disorders can affect both sexes. These can affect sperm quality and production in men, and can also affect egg quality, ovarian reserve in women. 
  • What types of genetic tests are available for fertility issues? 
Some common genetic tests for fertility issues include  karyotype analysis, whole exome sequencing, Y-chromosome microdeletion analysis, CFTR gene mutation testing, and preimplantation genetic testing (PGT).  
  • Is infertility inherited from parents? 
Yes, it is possible. According to estimates, genetic disorders can be a contributory factor in around 30% cases of infertility.  
  • How do chromosomal abnormalities affect reproductive health? 
These can lead to birth defects, recurrent miscarriages, and infertility. Such issues can occur due to errors during the formation of sperm or during early embryonic development.  
  • Can genetic counselling help couples facing infertility? 
Yes, it is possible, especially while considering ART fertility treatments. It can be useful in identifying underlying genetic factors that might cause fertility challenges. Couples can benefit from counselling and get a proper idea about the kind of reproductive options that they have.   The guidance and tips of infertility specialists can go a long way to help single patients and couples to understand the role of genetics in infertility. They can maximize their chances of pregnancy with the right steps.
