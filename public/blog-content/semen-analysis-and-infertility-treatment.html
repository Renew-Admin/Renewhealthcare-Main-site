What Is Semen: Semen Meaning

Semen is the thick fluid that comes from men’s penis when they ejaculate during sexual activity. It carries sperm out of a man’s body so it can fertilize an egg and create an embryo (the first stage of pregnancy).

What Is Semen Analysis Test/Sperm Motility Test

Semen analysis is a test of a man’s sperm and semen. Also known as a male sperm count test or male fertility test, its results show how many sperm are released, as well as how they’re shaped and how well they move. If you and your partner are having trouble getting pregnant, one of the first tests your doctor will likely ask for is a semen analysis. While both men and women can have problems, issues with male fertility can play a part in as many as half of all infertility cases. Male infertility is often caused by low sperm production. You’ll probably be asked to ejaculate into a collection cup in a private room at your doctor’s office.

Common Semen Analysis Terms To Know

When you look at your sperm analysis report, some of the terms can feel a bit technical at first. Here’s a quick breakdown so you’re not left out guessing what it all means:
  • Asthenospermia: This means the sperm are not moving well. Since movement is key for reaching the egg, low motility can affect fertility.
  • Azoospermia: This refers to a complete absence of sperm in the semen. In simple terms, no sperm are found in the sample.
  • Oligospermia: This means the sperm count is lower than normal. If the total motile sperm count drops below 20 million, it’s considered low. When it falls below 1 million, it’s considered severe.
  • Pyospermia: This condition indicates a high number of white blood cells in the semen, often linked to infection or inflammation, usually from the prostate.
  • Teratozoospermia: This refers to sperm that have an abnormal shape, which can make it harder for them to fertilize an egg.
  • Zona Pellucida: This is the outer layer of the egg. Sperm need to attach to and penetrate this layer for fertilization to happen.
You might see a combination of these terms in one semen report, and that’s completely normal. Doctors look at the full picture before suggesting the next steps.

What Is Semen Analysis Test Known As

A semen analysis test report is a detailed test for sperm count and quality. It checks:
  • How many sperm are present
  • How well they move (motility)
  • How they look (morphology)
Doctors usually recommend a sperm quality test as a first step when couples are trying to conceive but not seeing results. Here’s the honest bit. Male factors like sperm count and motility test contribute to nearly half of infertility cases. So this test is not optional, but essential.

When is Semen Examination Done

A semen examination is usually recommended when there's a need to understand male fertility better or confirm the success of a medical procedure. In most cases, doctors suggest sperm testing when a couple has been trying to conceive without success, as it helps assess sperm count, movement, and overall quality. It is also done after certain procedures to ensure everything is working as it should.

Infertility

If pregnancy isn’t happening despite regular attempts, a male fertility test like semen analysis becomes one of the first steps. It helps identify issues related to sperm count, motility, or structure that may be affecting conception.

Vasectomy Followup

After a vasectomy, a sperm count test is done to confirm that no sperm are present in the semen. This ensures the procedure was successful and prevents unintended pregnancy.

Semen Analysis Test Details

If you’re wondering how semen analysis is done, it’s actually a simple and well-controlled process. The most common way to collect a sample is through masturbation, either at home or in a private room at the clinic. You’ll be given a sterile container, and it’s important not to touch the inside or contaminate the sample. Since sperm count can vary from day to day, doctors may ask for more than one sample over a few weeks for accurate semen analysis results. Once your sample reaches the lab, specialists study it under a microscope for semen analysis procedure. This will provide a wealth of information, including:
  1. How many sperm are there (concentration)? A normal sperm count is at least 15 million sperm per milliliter of semen. Your sperm count is considered low if you have less than that.
  2. How are your sperm moving (motility)? Your doctor will look at how many sperm are moving and how well they move. Ideally, 50% or more of your sperm sample should be active.
  3. What does your sperm look like (morphology)? The size and shape of your sperm affect how well they can fertilize an egg. Normal semen will have at least 4% normally shaped sperm.
  4. In addition to analyzing your sperm, your doctor will also find out other details from your sample, including volume. They’ll note how much semen you were able to provide for your sample. A normal amount is at least 1.5 milliliters.
  5. If your sample is less than that, it could mean that your seminal vesicles aren’t making enough fluid or are blocked. You could also have a problem with your prostate.

Can Your Spouse Or Partner Help With Semen Analysis Test Procedure

Yes, your partner can be involved in helping you provide a semen sample. In some cases, couples prefer collecting the sample during intercourse using a special non-lubricated condom, which can then be transferred to a sterile container. That said, there are a few things to be careful about. Unprotected intercourse, saliva, or any kind of lubricant can interfere with the sperm quality results and affect your sperm analysis report. So if you’re going this route, it has to be done exactly as advised by the doctors.

How To Do Sperm Count Test At Home

Home-based sperm count tests are now available. They help with basic checking sperm count, but they don’t replace a full lab-based semen analysis. If you’re serious about fertility, lab testing is still the gold standard.

What Is The Normal Range For Sperm Analysis

The semen analysis normal range typically includes:
  • Sperm count: 15 million/ml or more
  • Motility: 40-50% active sperm
  • Morphology: Minimum 4% normal forms
  • Volume: 1.5 ml or higher

A normal sperm report doesn’t guarantee pregnancy, but it does improve the chances.
