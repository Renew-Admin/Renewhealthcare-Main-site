The world is moving faster than ever in 2025. With changing social dynamics, longer working hours and digital advancements reshaping modern lifestyle, these changes are undoubtedly affecting male and female fertility in a big way. Both sexes are experiencing fertility-related issues and suffering from reproductive difficulties at a noticeably higher rate than men and women in previous decades. Take a look at how lifestyle changes today are having a negative impact on male and female fertility, and what can be done to address these issues.

1. Increased Stress Levels and Career Pressures

When it comes to hormonal imbalances in the body, stress has always been a contributing factor. However, various studies have revealed that stress is reaching alarming heights in 2025. With both sexes working for longer hours, juggling hybrid and remote work models and experiencing performance-anxiety issues, their cortisol levels are increasing like never before. And this increase is consistent. Women can suffer from conditions like Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS), anovulation, irregular menstrual cycles and other issues due to chronic stress. Men may suffer from reduced sperm count, lower testosterone levels and affected sperm mortality. Both men and women suffering from fertility issues should consult an experienced infertility specialist in Kolkata or an IVF fertility specialist to evaluate their stress levels and get recommendations about lifestyle modifications, apart from seeking medical treatments.

2. Sedentary Lifestyle and Screen Addiction

It is true that there has been a drastic drop in physical activity levels with the rise of online entertainment, remote offices and AI-driven jobs. In 2025, people are spending around 10 to 12 hours in front of screens every day. This lack of movement is leading to obesity, reduced circulation of blood and nutrients throughout the body and metabolic disorders. All these are known to reduce fertility in both sexes. Men who sit for prolonged duration, especially with laptops on their laps, can suffer from raised scrotal temperature, which can have a negative impact on their sperm quality. This is the reason why dietary counseling and physical fitness are part of the fertility plans of many IVF clinics in Kolkata, where doctors understand how deep hormonal balance is associated with physical activity levels and proper diet. In 2025, an increasing number of people are suffering from irregular meal timings. They are depending more on takeaways and processed foods due to their fast-paced lifestyle. While these diets are convenient, they are often high in preservatives, trans fats, and sugar. These lack key nutrients such as iron, folate, zinc, and antioxidants, each crucial for supporting optimal reproductive health. 5. Environmental and Digital Pollution

As per emerging studies, digital radiation and environmental pollutants are harming reproductive health more in 2026 than ever before in history. As men and women are being constantly exposed to electromagnetic fields from mobile towers, WiFi and gadgets, they are experiencing a steady decline in sperm motility and ovarian function. Toxins in cleaning products and skincare items, endocrine-disrupting chemicals in plastics, and air pollution – these are also harming hormonal balance in the body. 6. Increased Prevalence of Mental Health Disorders

Reproductive health is closely linked to an individual’s mental and emotional well-being. Mental health conditions like depression, anxiety, and consequent sleep disorders are getting increasingly prevalent in 2026, affecting hormonal balance and reproductive health. Many more men and women are relying on anti-anxiety medications and antidepressants, which can sometimes interfere with menstrual regularity, sperm production and libido.
