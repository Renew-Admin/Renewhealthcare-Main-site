What Is Birth Control? 

Birth control is a technique that helps to prevent unintended pregnancies and the health risks associated with the same, including problems like poor, physical health and maternal mortality. It is also referred to as contraception. 

Different types of methods, devices or medications can be used for preventing pregnancy. Broadly, such methods can be classified into: 

  • Barrier methods,  
  • Hormonal methods,  
  • Intrauterine devices (IUDs),  
  • Sterilization, and  
  • Behavioral methods 

Birth control helps to avoid childbirth by either preventing conception (the fertilization of an egg by a sperm) or interfering with implantation or growth of an egg.  

With effective birth control, you can choose when to have kids and whether you should have them at all. You can space out your pregnancies and reduce any need to abort your child in an unsafe way.  

With some birth control methods, you can also protect yourself against sexually transmitted infections (STIs).

Myths About Birth Control 

Just as you need to stay safe from STIs, you should also protect yourself from being affected by some myths that commonly surround birth control, and find out the truth.  

Myth: Birth control damages fertility 

This is completely unfounded. No evidence exists that show long-term fertility can be affected by birth control methods like hormonal contraceptives, as some people believe commonly.  

Myth: Birth control pills must be stopped every once in a while 

Some people feel that regular use of these pills can be damaging for fertility and overall health. This, again, is absolutely untrue. No medical reason exists that support why people need to take a break when confirmed under medical supervision. 

Myth: Birth control pills are only useful for avoiding pregnancy 

This is not true either. As any IVF doctor in Kolkata will tell you, these types of medications, other than helping women to prevent pregnancies, can also help treat acne problems, PCOS reduce PMS symptoms, and manage menstrual cycle irregularities.  

Myth: Birth control pills lead to delayed return to fertility or infertility  

Without any scientific research studies or reports to back up such claims, many people promote such things needlessly.  

Myth: IUDs can lead to Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) 

The use of IUDs does not lead to PID. Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) occurs only rarely and it is typically a result of STIs or sexually transmitted infections.  

Myth: Birth control can help prevent STIs 

This is another common myth about these pills, which has no scientific basis. As a matter of fact, protection from STIs can only be possible with the use of condoms.  

Myth: Natural birth control methods do not yield results 

If someone says that these are ineffective, do not believe him. The Rhythm method, or Fertility awareness, has 76-88% effectiveness. The withdrawal method, on the other hand, can be 78% effective, when used in a proper way.  

Conclusion 

As you can clearly understand, many myths surround the use of birth control methods. However, these are untrue and women who want to use these techniques should refrain from believing such baseless suppositions.  

FAQ – Frequently Asked Questions about Birth Control 

  • Are birth control pills used before IVF? 

Yes, according to the best IVF center in Kolkata, these are often used before the IVF process for menstrual cycle regulation, preventing premature ovulation and possible improvement of egg yield.  

  • Can birth control methods stop my period? 

Yes, these can reduce your period significantly or even stop it. With continuous or extended use of methods such as hormonal birth control pills, implants and injections, you can prevent or delay your periods. 

  • Does birth control come with any risks? 

Only a small percentage of people taking the combination (estrogen-containing) birth control pill can suffer from rare complications like high blood pressure, Pulmonary embolism and Deep vein thrombosis (DVT).  Thus, they must be started after proper evaluation by a doctor to reduce any associated risk. 

  • Which is the safest birth control method? 

Long-acting reversible contraceptives (LARCs), including IUDs and implants, are the safest and most effectual birth control methods for preventing pregnancy.
